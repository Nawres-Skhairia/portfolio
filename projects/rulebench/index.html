Rulebench

It finds the loopholes in a company's rules before employees do.

  1. 1 · The new rule

    A sales manager writes: Every sale under 10,000 earns an extra 400 bonus, to reward small new customers.

  2. 2 · The trick

    A salesperson with one 30,000 sale writes it up as three sales of 9,900 to the same customer, and collects the bonus three times. Nobody notices for months.

    30,000→9,9009,9009,900+1,200 bonus
  3. 3 · Rulebench

    Before the rule is used, it plays the clever employee and tries thousands of tricks. It shows which ones work, what they would cost, and the fix. It also flags the cases the rule forgot and replays last year's real sales to show the cost.

      How it works

      Rules as a decision table

      Each rule set is a decision table, the format business analysts use in DMN. Every row has conditions and a result, and exactly one row should apply to each case. You can edit any cell and every test re-runs instantly.

      Coverage

      The engine cuts the space of possible cases at every boundary used in the table, then checks each piece. A piece no row covers is a gap. A piece where two rows give different answers is a conflict.

      Red team

      An adversary plays the person the rule applies to. It may only make moves that person really controls, like splitting a deal or moving a signing date, and it searches for the combination that pays the most. Nothing it finds is random: every exploit comes with a concrete example.

      Replay

      Last year's real cases go through today's rules and the draft side by side, so you see the cost and the workload of the change before you announce it.

      Demo data is fictional: names, companies and amounts are generated. Everything runs in this browser and nothing is sent to a server. Your edits are kept in this browser only.
