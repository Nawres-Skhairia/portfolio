Functional and technical QA across the full factoring lifecycle: third-party onboarding and compliance screening, contracts and credit limits, invoicing and funding, payments and banking interfaces (SEPA, cross-border, ISO 20022, MT940, CAMT.053), cash allocation, collections, accounting and night jobs.   1. Current

    Functional QA & Business Analyst, iMX Factoring Solutions · Codix

    2025–today

    iMX, enterprise factoring and receivables finance platform for banks and factoring companies

    Functional QA: factoring lifecycle

    • Third parties and onboarding: creation of individuals and legal entities and assignment of their roles (client, debtor, guarantor), with sanctions and compliance screening.
    • Contracts: set-up of each factoring product (recourse and non-recourse, disclosed and undisclosed, reverse factoring), amendments, renewals and termination.
    • Limits and credit risk: debtor limit requests through the approval workflow (approval, partial approval, refusal, expiry, renewal), limit consumption, over-limit and concentration scenarios, and their impact on funding.
    • Portfolio management: client and debtor portfolios, outstanding balances, risk exposure and portfolio transfers.
    • Invoicing and assignment: intake of invoices and credit notes by screen and file import, eligibility rules, rejections, and invoicing of the factor's fees and commissions.
    • Funding: availability, advance rate and reserve, funding requests, payment orders and cut-off times; fee and interest calculations validated against contract terms, including period-end, rate-change and minimum-fee scenarios.
    • Multi-currency: invoicing, funding and payments across currencies, exchange rates and currency conversion of balances.
    • Financial items: creation, life cycle and balances of the financial items generated by invoices, payments, fees and adjustments.
    • Cash allocation: allocation of debtor payments to invoices, covering partial, over- and underpayments, splits, unidentified payments, reallocations, reversals and write-offs.
    • Collections and disputes: dunning cycles, overdue tracking and ageing, recourse on unpaid items, and dispute handling from opening to resolution, with impact on eligibility and funding.
    • Accounting: accounting impact of each business event (assignment, funding, allocation, fees, write-off) and reconciliation of client and debtor balances.
    • Reporting and statements: client and debtor statements, notices, letters and operational reports, reconciled against the database.
    • User rights: roles, permissions, four-eyes approval and audit trail.
    • Configuration: validation of business flows under client- and country-specific parameters.

    Technical QA

    • Payments and banking interfaces: validation of outgoing SEPA and cross-border payment files (ISO 20022 XML) and incoming MT940 and CAMT.053 bank statements, end to end from bank file to client account.
    • Night jobs: interest accrual, ageing, limit expiry, dunning and file processing, verified in the database and logs, including month-end, value-date and rerun-after-failure scenarios.
    • APIs and web services: REST API and web service testing with Postman, including consistency of records and accounting between API and user interface.
    • Data validation: SQL queries to verify balances, accounting entries and processing results against expected outcomes.
    • Investigation: root-cause analysis using SQL and application logs.

    Business analysis and test management

    • Requirements analysis: review of business requirements and software specifications to detect gaps and inconsistencies before development; definition of test scenarios and acceptance criteria with business experts and client teams.
    • Test design: positive, negative and boundary test cases, end-to-end scenarios across the factoring lifecycle, and supporting test data.
    • Execution and release validation: test execution across DEV, VAL and UAT; smoke testing, retesting, and full regression and non-regression campaigns ahead of each release.
    • Test and defect management: test cases and campaigns in Jira and Xray; evidence-based defect reports (reproduction steps, expected and actual results, SQL results, log extracts) followed through to closure with development.

    AI testing

    • Dedicated tester of an AI solution: evaluation of answers for accuracy, consistency, hallucinations and high-risk cases.
    • Design of test question sets with expected answers; reporting of quality issues to the product team.
